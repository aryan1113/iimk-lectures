# Sessions 7-9: Organizational Inertia and Responses to Success and Failure

## Key Concepts

- **Action inertia**: the tendency to keep following established patterns of behaviour. **Active inertia** exists when the pull of the past is strong (examining success is as necessary as examining failure).
- **Why practices are sticky:** they are the flip side of predictability; path of least resistance; well known and comfortable; **social complexity including causal ambiguity** (it is unclear why things work, because they arise from interpersonal relations or culture).
- **Ingrained culture**: "Culture eats strategy for breakfast" (Peter Drucker).
- **Leadership failures**: as organizations grow, the talents that took them to their current state often become detrimental to future viability.
- **Inertia and evolution**: Darwin was right that neither strength nor intelligence guarantees survival; only adaptation does, for firms as for flora and fauna (O'Reilly, Harreld and Tushman).
- **Responses to low performance:**
  - **Problemistic (problem-solving) search**: search driven by a problem and aimed at fixing low performance; brings innovative behaviour and risk-taking.
  - **Downward spiral**: self-perpetuating system with its own momentum.
- **Responses to high performance:**
  - **Complacency**: attribution bias (outside the slides: crediting success to oneself), overconfidence, failure to ask why success happened.
  - **Slack search**: search driven by excess resources; innovative behaviour but limited risk-taking.

"Why do we fall? So we can learn to pick ourselves up."
- **Managerial implications:**
  - Conducive organizational design with institutionalized processes: introspection, leadership turnover, experimentation.
  - **Psychological safety.**
  - **Setting reference points**: absolute measures are less meaningful; use historical and social indicators (peers).
  - **Organizational learning** examples: continuous improvement at Toyota, growth reference points at Microsoft.
- **Advice for TMT/consultants:**
  - *Low performers*: stabilize the **panic paradigm** (desperate managers seeking a silver bullet or erratic cost-cutting); do rigorous analysis and redirect from knee-jerk reactions.
  - *High performers*: distinguish current success from future viability; deal with complacent managers resisting intrusions by creating appropriate **trigger points** and highlighting blind spots.
  - Consultant X-Files lesson: managing data or strategy is the easy part; understanding the organizational state is key: **calm the panicked low performer and wake up the comfortable high performer.**

## Why This Matters

Organizations fail by holding on to what worked or by panicking; the right intervention depends on whether performance is low or high.

## Relevance for Exams / Cases

Classify a firm's response (problemistic search, spiral, complacency, slack search), explain the source of inertia, and advise differently for a struggling vs thriving firm.
