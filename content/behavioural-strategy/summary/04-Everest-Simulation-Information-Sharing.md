# Sessions 10-12: Everest Simulation Debrief, Group Information Sharing and Process Losses

## Key Concepts

- **What the deck is:** a debrief of the **Everest simulation** (a simulation (outside the slides: a team exercise on climbing Everest)). The slides give only headings (Results, Debrief), with no scenario or scoring detail, so the lessons are the debrief themes below.
- **Learning goals of the debrief:**
  - Problem-solving and decision-making in teams whose members have **different information and opposing interests**.
  - How teams handle trade-offs between **short-term task completion and longer-term team effectiveness**, including not reaching their potential.
  - How **process losses** (outside the slides: value lost to the way a team works) can be minimized in organizational learning.
- **What hampers effective information sharing** (information not shared, or shared but not processed):
  - Absence of shared goals, including conflicting ones.
  - Information overload.
  - Not realizing others do not have your information.
  - **Groupthink** (named on the slide; definition outside the slides: pressure for agreement over critical thinking).
  - Discussing shared information feels good, is easier, and makes one look competent and credible.
  - Lack of psychological safety; group inertia; a history of dysfunctional debates or decision rules; hierarchy or status differences; recipient and source characteristics; individual biases and agendas.
- **Common information effect:** information held by more members before discussion has more influence on team judgement than information held by fewer. Groups spend too little time on **unshared (unique) information**, and even when it is shared they fail to capture it in the decision. Consequence: teams often do worse than some of their individual performers.
- **Organizational responses to process losses:**
  - Put **processes** in place: explicit forms (SOPs, knowledge management systems) and implicit forms (shadowing, mentoring).
  - **Reduce over-dependence on individuals**: decision logs, cross-training, job rotation.
  - Role of **leadership or an information manager** to focus the group and encourage sharing of unique information.
  - Reduce conflict and raise psychological safety.
  - Integrate AI tools.

## Why This Matters

Shows that teams hold the right information yet fail to use it, so structure and leadership are needed to turn distributed knowledge into decisions.

## Relevance for Exams / Cases

Diagnose why a team decision ignored a member's unique knowledge (common information effect, hierarchy, low psychological safety) and propose fixes.
