# Sessions 14-15: Organizational Learning Context and Wisdom of the Collective

## Key Concepts

- **Context matters for organizational learning.** The deck shows the growth of the **quaternary sector** (asks about the entertainment sector; no definition given) and lists items that support organizational learning (image-only slides, not readable), leading to **new business models and new ways of organizing**.
- **Revisiting Everest:** information is not shared, or shared information is not processed. **Common information effect**: information held by more members has more influence than information held by fewer; groups spend too little time on unshared information and fail to capture it even when shared.
- **The other side: collective intelligence effect.** Strategic decisions (entering a new country, starting a new business line) should integrate divergent knowledge and expertise with deep understanding of context; "easy to put in a PowerPoint, hard to put into practice".
- **What managers attempt vs achieve:**
  - Diverse knowledge brought together vs reality of the common information effect interacting with **HIPPO** (acronym not expanded on the slides; commonly read as highest paid person's opinion, outside the slides)).
  - Active discussion and problem-solving vs unequal or no participation (no views sought from the top, no feedback from the bottom).
  - Proactive exploration vs reactive, fragmented efforts.
  - Question: how to unlock latent cognitive diversity and turn it into organizational intelligence?
- **Wisdom of the collective** ("none of us is as smart as all of us"). Galton at the fair: the median guess of an ox's weight was within 1%. Success stories listed: Napoleon to Tipu Sultan and the BP oil spill (titles only, no detail), internet-based models such as Wikipedia, LEGO Ideas (most-voted ideas become products), Stack Overflow, Quora, GoFundMe, Google reviews.
- **"And yet.." slide** lists, without explanation: Shawshank Redemption (initial box-office failure; asks which movie held the top spot before), the 2008 Godfather vs Dark Knight rivalry, **New Coke 1985** (shrinking gap with Pepsi; 2,00,000 blind taste tests), community-led village courts (witch trials to stoning), betting and financial markets. The slide's theme appears to be that collective judgement can go wrong (inferred; the slides do not spell out the outcomes).
- **Conditions for the wisdom of crowds:**
  - **Diversity** of the crowd (absence of similarities).
  - **Independence** of individuals (no shared agenda, not influencing each other).
  - **Ability to aggregate** the information.
  - Individual value-claiming must not crowd out group value-creation.
- **When it does not work:** missing the three conditions; lack of expertise (medicine, law); need for quick decisions; confidentiality or privacy concerns; lack of incentives to participate.
- **Designing around challenges:** **Google** uses closed betting until a set time for employee feedback on business ideas (independence, avoids groupthink); **Threadless** filters community-voted designs through internal staff selection (reduces noise); **Netflix** retro-fits crowd-generated preferences into its internal model (diversity).
- **Class activity:** design a question whose answer needs the class's collective wisdom, greater than the sum of individual contributions.

## Why This Matters

Groups can be smarter or dumber than individuals; outcomes depend on diversity, independence and aggregation design.

## Relevance for Exams / Cases

Judge whether a crowd or team will be wise given the three conditions, and propose design fixes (Google, Threadless, Netflix style).
