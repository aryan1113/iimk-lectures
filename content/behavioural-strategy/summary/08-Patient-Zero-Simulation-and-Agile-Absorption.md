# Sessions 19-20: Patient Zero Simulation, Strategic Decision-Making and Agile Absorption

## Key Concepts

- **What the deck is:** a debrief of the **Patient Zero simulation**, in which teams (inferred: each running a country facing an outbreak) decide on five events: religious pilgrimage, Insight technology test kits, Bilbring and Hite's ineffective vaccine, Zephyr Computing's hidden software, bombing a cluster of the infected. The slides give no scoring or outcome detail ("Results" is a title only); debrief questions: could you save your country, what information did you wish for, how did you handle dissenters, which event was hardest.
- **Difficulties of decision-making in implementation:** imperfect choices, incomplete information, no prior experience, pressure from outside events, and trade-offs and tensions (religious freedom vs public health, identity challenges, is bombing ever right?). Implementation requires deciding despite uncertainty about correctness and facing very hard challenges, with significant consequences.
- **Deferring decisions:** should strategic decisions be deferred? "The world moves on"; decisions get revisited.
- **Why are results bad even after good choices?** Information is scarce, contradictory or still developing; bounded rationality; limited control when many stakeholders implement; no control over public response; (almost always) a different context; missing precedent. Also ask whether the choices were really good. A "seminal view on strategy implementation" slide is an image only (outside the slides: likely Mintzberg's emergent strategy, which is consistent with the question about crafting emergent strategies).
- **Which organizational attributes help recovery** and crafting emergent strategies, to rebound from bad decisions?
- **Ali vs Foreman.** Muhammad Ali ("float like a butterfly, sting like a bee") epitomized **agility**; George Foreman ("take a licking and keep on ticking") epitomized **absorption**. At the 1974 Rumble in the Jungle (about one billion viewers, $100m revenue; records shown as 40-0 (37 KO) vs 44-2 (31 KO), which fighter has which is outside the slides) Ali won by **agile absorption**: kept his agility but built absorption (clobbered by sparring partners, rope-a-dope) and KO'd Foreman in round 8.
- **Agile absorption in a firm** (resilience under uncertainty):
  - **Structural factors:** low fixed costs; a mix of tangible (raw materials, real estate) and intangible (brand, technology) resources; diversified cash flows; customer lock-in or protected core market; **slack resources** (more than required).
  - **Behavioural factors:** disciplined decision-making (awareness, processes, constructive challenge); continuous learning and improvement (learning organization); leadership fluidity (evolving with the organization); preventing action inertia, downward spiral and complacency; rebounding from crisis; empowerment and fair process.
- **Key takeaways:** embrace reality (work around predicted models, make tough choices); no one-size-fits-all models in novel, information-poor situations; cultivate the ability to respond and become comfortable with the discomfort of winning and losing, trade-offs and identity challenges; build resilience through agile absorption.

## Why This Matters

Good decisions can still produce bad outcomes, so resilience (structural plus behavioural) matters more than prediction.

## Relevance for Exams / Cases

Explain why a well-reasoned implementation failed, and recommend structural and behavioural agile-absorption measures for a firm.
