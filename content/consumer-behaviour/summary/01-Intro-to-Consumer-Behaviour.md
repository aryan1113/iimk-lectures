# Introduction to Consumer Behaviour

## Key Concepts

- **Definition** (Solomon): the study of the processes involved when an individual or group selects, purchases, uses and disposes of a product, service or experience to satisfy needs. Core questions: what, why, when, where, how often they buy and use.
- **Drucker's view**: the only valid purpose of a business is to create a customer; the customer determines what the business is.
- **Why study CB**: at the *individual* level (you are a consumer in every role; purchases shape standard of living); at the *managerial* level (successful marketing decisions need CB information; CB can't guarantee success but ignoring it leads to disaster). Decisions are *micro* (help the firm meet its objectives, e.g. Korean firms' consumer insights) and *macro* (consumers collectively shape society and economy).
- **Evolution of orientation**: sales concept (profit via volume) → product concept (profit via quality) → modern marketing / consumer-oriented concept (profit via satisfaction). CB emerged because consumers do not act as marketing theory predicts; it is complex and multidimensional ("easier said than done").
- **Consumer focus spreads to services**: patient-centric hospitals, student-centric schools, account-holder banks.
- **Drivers of change in India**: booming economy, young market (about half under 25), smaller and nuclear households, DINKs, small SKUs, awareness, westernisation. **Trends**: affordable indulgence (EMI over absolute price), time pressure, health consciousness, upgrading vs lifelong possession, convenience.
- **Consumer orientation**: give the right products to consumers rather than finding consumers for the product; creating value in a way competitors cannot; a culture of consumer orientation, competitor orientation and inter-functional coordination.
- **Customer centricity**: a core value, not an event. Use customer data, commit to customer success, engage from the start, commit top-down, design processes from the customer's view, measure what matters to customers, encourage co-creation. The 1990s shift from company-driven (push, excess demand) to consumer-driven (pull, excess supply) economy; firms became market-driven rather than marketing-driven.
- **Curiosity** as a marketer's virtue (with empathy and acumen): the gap between what we know and want to know; yields deep insight and continuous improvement. Tools: keep updating knowledge, listen without judgement, always ask, observe with fresh eyes, seek new experiences, embrace the unexpected.
- **Disruption** (Borders, Nokia, J.Crew): the consumer, not technology, is the disruptor, so firms must move from firm- or competitor-centric to consumer-centric.
- **Consumer value chain**: need arousal → information search → evaluation of alternatives → choice → purchase. Now **decoupled**: showrooming (visit the store, scan barcodes, buy cheaper online; Best Buy case).

## Why This Matters

Frames the whole course: consumers are central, complex and changing, and firms win by understanding them rather than pushing products.

## Relevance for Exams / Cases

Be ready to define CB, trace the evolution of marketing orientation, list customer-centricity practices, and explain showrooming and decoupling of the value chain.
