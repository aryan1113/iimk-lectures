# Emotion in Consumer Behaviour

## Key Concepts

- **Emotion** is a subjective feeling state from consciously or subconsciously evaluating an event relevant to a goal. Positive emotion appears when the goal is advanced, negative when it is impeded (the salesperson who nearly closes the annual quota exercise).
- **Eight primary emotions** (with families): anger, sadness, fear, joy, interest (acceptance, trust, love), surprise, disgust, shame (guilt, embarrassment, regret). **Secondary emotions** are reactions to an emotion (shame about anger); emotions can also be grouped as active/passive positive and negative.
- **Multicomponent model**: emotions are coordinated changes in cognitive appraisal, bodily symptoms, action tendencies, expression and feelings. Emotions have intensity, direction and quality; they are everyday, varied and sometimes culture-specific; hard to identify and harder to articulate.
- **Delivering emotion in the market**: *product emotions* come from product attributes; *associated emotions* are generated independently (through words, images, contexts) and become linked to the product through repetition (Surf Excel ads).
- **Emotional marketing levers**: storytelling, visuals, music and sound, personalisation, humour, social proof, cause marketing, nostalgia, fear and urgency, empathy.
- **Fear appeals**: loss-framed mammogram messages got more screening than gain-framed ones (the sunscreen slide gives the two wordings without a result); explained by Rogers' **Protection Motivation Theory** (threat severity, probability, availability of a coping response, ability to cope).
- **Shame and guilt in marketing**: condoms and sexual wellness (discreet delivery, D2C brands), baby products (vaguely or outright deceptive claims, e.g. formula vs breast milk), fairness creams. **Beyond funeral campaign**: buyers are vulnerable and want to finish quickly, lack knowledge and price transparency, face social norms against price shopping, and sellers can guilt them into a pricier version; the campaign tried to break those norms.
- **Implications for CB**: builds desire and urge, hedonic, impulse and variety-seeking purchases, and loyalty. Marketer's role: reduce the *thinking factor* (easy process, optimal choice, less information), reduce the *risk factor* (safe store or brand), raise the *impression factor* (memorable encounters).
- **Why it matters**: Emory fMRI Coke vs Pepsi study; when brands were identified the limbic system (emotion, memory, unconscious) lit up more, so brand emotion shapes response beyond the product itself.

## Why This Matters

Emotion drives much of consumption beyond rational evaluation; marketers can design associated emotions.

## Relevance for Exams / Cases

Distinguish product from associated emotions, apply Protection Motivation Theory to a fear appeal, and critique an emotional campaign (fear, shame, guilt, deceptive baby-product claims).
