# Neuroscience and Marketing (Neuromarketing)

## Key Concepts

- **What it is**: an indirect, non-verbal approach that analyses the brain's reactions to stimuli and applies the findings to make marketing more effective; it measures attention with emotional impact and what drives likability and memorability (retention and retrieval).
- **Why neuroscience**: marketers moved from big data and small data to *brain data*. Self-reports mislead: in a narrative-engagement study, people said they were more engaged by visual narration while skin conductance and heart rate told a different story (the deck's explanation points to audio: we simulate character and context, video is passive). "What comes out of our mouths is not a perfect rendition of what's going on in our brains."
- **Self-report vs neurophysiological measures**: self-report is cheap, simple, less intrusive, but conscious, retrospective, and prone to recall bias, social desirability, order effects, over/under-reporting and the *peak-end* effect. Neuro measures are real-time, automatic, capture conscious and subconscious fluctuation across the journey, and separate **valence** (positive/negative) from **arousal**, but are more intrusive and costly. Neuro research is *subconscious*; traditional research is *conscious*.
- **Tools**:
  - **Facial coding**: emotions show on the face (broadly universal, culture and intensity vary); software (clmtrackr, Affectiva) tracks facial landmarks. Manual face reading depends on the individual and may be inaccurate. Captures valence, not arousal.
  - **Eye tracking**: exploration (eye movement) → consideration (fixations) → choice (arousal); attention via fixations, arousal via pupil dilation; measures gaze direction, what grabs or confuses attention, speed of recognition; outputs heat maps (warmer colour where more people fixate), time to first fixation, fixation duration and count. Uses: website design, shelf and product placement, pricing, package and ad testing. Taco Bell: bundle ads above the POS (where people look while waiting) lifted sales ~40% and order size. A model looking straight out of the ad draws attention to the face, so have them look at what you want noticed.
  - **fMRI**: tracks oxygenated blood flow to active brain regions. *Smoking warnings*: self-reports said warnings worked, but fMRI showed activation of the nucleus accumbens (craving/reward) and the insula (disgust), and the warnings made smokers look away, so the information never reaches them. The deck then tests warning formats (none, words, self-directed image, other-directed image) but states no conclusion; the chart suggests image warnings cut the measured response. *Wine pricing*: the same wine labelled $45 vs $5 was reported and measured (orbitofrontal cortex, pleasantness) as more enjoyable, while taste regions were unaffected; price adds associated emotional value beyond taste. Price shown before vs after exposure triggers different value calculations. Shiny chip bags activate the guilt-linked anterior cingulate; matte "healthy" bags don't.
  - **EEG**: scalp electrodes record neuron electrical activity; good for attention, arousal, fatigue, surprise, engagement and recall.
  - **Galvanic skin response (GSR)**: skin conductance from sweating shows arousal (predicts attention and memory) but not valence; complements facial coding. Blush response is an emerging tool.
  - **Tone and voice analysis**: reviews and calls (Cogito scores energy, interruption, empathy, participation, tone, pace for real-time customer-service guidance).
- **Future: neuro-nudges**. A *nudge* is a simple change in the decision environment that predictably alters behaviour without forbidding options or changing incentives. Examples: *sleep nudging* (smokers exposed to cigarette smell plus rotten egg during early sleep smoked less), *hormone manipulation* (testosterone raised preference for luxury brands, hypothesised as status signalling), *TMS temporary neural inhibition* (magnetic stimulation switches off brain areas, used to study fear/disgust and openness to novel foods such as insect snacks). The deck concludes this generates excitement and ethical concerns.

## Why This Matters

Consumers cannot always explain their own preferences; neuro tools reveal attention, arousal and value that surveys miss.

## Relevance for Exams / Cases

Choose the right tool for a research question, contrast self-report with neuro measures, and discuss the wine and smoking-warning findings and ethical limits.
