# Beyond Meat: Attitudes and Behaviour Change (Case)

## Key Concepts

- **The case**: Beyond Meat sells plant-based meat and wants to change behaviour through learning, not force: attitude → intention → behaviour. India context: consumers favour fresh home-cooked food; plant-based protein is small, low-penetration and growing fast (large FMCG players and startups entering).
- **What founders want from consumers**: to see a product as protein whether from animal or plant; to understand the impact of animal products on environment, animals and humans and the food-body-health link; change through learning, not force.
- **Objectives**: serve three stakeholders (human, animal, environment); cut meat consumption; reduce animals killed; educate on benefits; save the environment (water and methane footprint of cattle).
- **FAB analysis**: *features* are meat-like taste, texture, smell, colour and sizzle, and none of the antibiotics, hormones, additives of processed meat; *benefits* are health (lifestyle disease, hormonal and mental health, longevity).
- **Attitude**: a learned predisposition to respond consistently favourably or unfavourably to an object. **Tricomponent model**: cognitive (knowledge and beliefs from experience and information; key to building product beliefs), affective (feelings; key for new products and unfamiliar brands, and in an established category positive or negative affect shapes brand attitude directly), conative (likelihood of acting). For a new category (air purifier, hand sanitiser, wooden houses) people move from *no attitude* to an attitude through learning, exposure and company communication, influenced by attitudes toward related products (animal → plant meat), concept awareness and liking, and trends ("plant forward").
- **Attitude-toward-object model**: attitude is a function of the presence and evaluation of attributes (focus on no chemicals, hormones, GMO; good for ecosystem; strong category traits of taste, texture, colour).
- **Intention-action gap**: liking plant meat but not buying it. **Attitude-toward-behaviour model** and **Theory of Reasoned Action** fix this: behaviour follows intention, built from attitude toward the behaviour (beliefs that the behaviour leads to outcomes, and evaluation of those outcomes) and subjective norm (beliefs about what referents think, and motivation to comply). Example: walking daily because it gives an ideal BMI and parents want it.
- **Attitude functions**: utilitarian (health primary; animal and eco welfare secondary), ego-defensive (protection from inner doubt; consumers are savvy and seek new solutions), value-expressive (lifestyle: "plant-based is cool", designer food), knowledge (need for cognition: food labelling showing bioavailability and environmental impact).
- **Changing attitudes**: exposé intervention (teen study: healthy eating framed as rebellion against manipulative food marketing raised appeal and some behaviour), resolve conflicting attitudes (taste vs animals/forests), offer craveable products and experience, change the relative evaluation of attributes (less chemical, more natural).
- **Segments**: Gen Z/millennials (spend on food experience), health- and environment-conscious, vegetarians, vegans, flexitarians, foodies, "lessitarians"; early adopters with high OSL and need for cognition. Watch growth rate per segment.
- **Marketing mix**: price higher than regular meat but below chicken strips (small scale); placed next to regular meat as substitute; price promotions questionable; social, broadcast and event promotion.
- **Future**: product extension (pork, fish, dried meat), flavours, sizes, CRM to track evolving preferences (the deck also names the Ansoff matrix, title only). **Competition**: Quorn and Gardein could not match Beyond's near-meat products; Impossible is the real competitor; also Kellogg's, Tyson, Nestlé, lab-grown meat and conventional producers. Focus areas: manufacturing, supply chain, taste evolution. Assignment: roadmap to launch alt-meat in India.

## Why This Matters

Shows how attitude theory (tricomponent, attitude toward object/behaviour, TORA, functions) is applied to launch a category that needs behaviour change.

## Relevance for Exams / Cases

Apply TORA and the attitude functions to a product, explain the intention-action gap, and recommend segments and an attitude-change strategy.
