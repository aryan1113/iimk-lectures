This handout uses Nykaa to show how marketing works in e-commerce: how customers move from need to purchase and repeat purchase, which channels you control or buy, how search ads are sold and ranked, and how a firm should join channels together and measure them. A caution about the source: the handout's diagrams were lost in conversion, leaving only titles, so some parts below are reconstructed and are flagged.

## Touch points and the customer decision journey

A **touch point** is every opportunity a customer has to see or hear about the brand, or to have an encounter or experience with it. Marketing is about managing those moments.

The **customer decision journey (CDJ)** has five steps: need recognition, information search, evaluation of alternatives, purchase decision and post-purchase behaviour. The handout also lists the purchase funnel, the McKinsey consumer-journey model and AIDA or hierarchy of effects (all as funnel analogies), but the diagrams are missing.

> **Extra context (not on the slides):** AIDA stands for attention, interest, desire, action. McKinsey's model is usually drawn as a loop of initial consideration, active evaluation, moment of purchase and post-purchase experience. The loop point is that a good experience feeds the next consideration. These expansions are outside the handout, which preserved only the titles.

## Owned, earned and paid media

The grouping below is reconstructed from a flattened table, so treat edge cases with care.

- **Owned media**: channels the brand controls: website, blog, newsletter, email, direct mail, online newsroom (the list also includes employee uniforms and logos on company vehicles).
- **Earned media**: exposure that others create: journalistic coverage, reviews and testimonials, word of mouth, user-generated content, likes and retweets.
- **Paid media**: exposure the brand buys: ads and commercials, sponsorship, pay-per-click, banner ads.

YouTube, Facebook and Twitter appear in the list as social channels, but where they sit is uncertain. The distinction to remember is control versus credibility versus cost: owned gives control, earned gives credibility but cannot be directed, paid buys reach on demand.

## The role of content

Content does three jobs for a beauty retailer. **Risk reduction**: reviews, blogs and vlogs and the Beauty Book help a shopper feel safe buying a product they cannot touch. **Social proof**: community comments and expert endorsement show others trust it. **Aspiration activation**: editorial content makes the customer want the look or lifestyle.

## Search: paid and organic

A search result page shows **paid links** (sponsored or search ads) and **organic links**. Search ads give digital visibility and target **intent**: they reach customers at the moment they express interest, so a higher return on investment is possible. The handout contrasts this with display ads and poses a question: is search "most likely to capture only existing demand?" The handout leaves it open and gives no answer.

### How search engine marketing works

In **search engine marketing**, advertisers purchase **keywords**, and their ad displays when a user's search matches. Two matching styles are listed: **exact or standard match** (the handout notes it is less expensive) and **broad or advanced match**. (Extra context: broad match is usually understood to cast a wider net, which is my gloss; the handout says no more.) The search engine is paid **on click**, not on display.

### The ad auction

Advertisers compete for limited space, and an **auction** decides which ads show and in which order (ad ranking). Two drivers matter:

- **Bid amount**: the maximum the advertiser will pay per click.
- **Ad quality or quality score**: keyword relevance and popularity (through links from other pages), the landing page, and ad extensions that add information.

(Extra context: so money is not the only driver, and a relevant ad can plausibly outrank a higher bid. The handout lists the two drivers but does not say how they combine.)

> **Extra context (not on the slides):** Suppose two advertisers bid on "face serum". One bids more but sends clicks to a generic home page; the other bids a bit less but lands users on a matching product page. Because the quality score counts, the second can rank higher and pay less per click. The numbers here are invented to show the idea.

## App strategy: repurchase and retention

For **repurchase** the handout lists replenishment reminders, relevant cross-selling, and reduced friction through saved preferences, order history, a wishlist and easy reordering. For **retention** it lists three tools: **personalisation** (recommendations from past purchases, browsing and stated preferences), **notifications** (push messages on offers, availability and replenishment) and **loyalty benefits** (points, member perks, exclusive offers). Retention is where lifetime value is built, a theme analytics returns to.

## SoMoLo

**SoMoLo** stands for **social** (discovery and influence through creators, reviews and sharing), **mobile** (convenient discovery and purchase through mobile-first design, the app and notifications) and **location** (geographically relevant targeting and offers). The handout stresses it is **not three separate channels**: the real question is how they reinforce one another, (Extra context: for instance, a creator video that opens the app with a nearby-delivery offer, an example of mine, not the handout's).

## Strategic integration across the funnel

The handout maps tools to four stages:

1. **Discovery**: SEO and paid search; the outcome is a site visit.
2. **Evaluation**: reviews, Beauty Book, blogs and videos; the outcome is understanding and consideration.
3. **Conversion**: product page, cart, checkout and offers; the outcome is purchase.
4. **Post-purchase and repeat**: order communication, notifications, recommendations and loyalty; the outcome is retention and higher lifetime value.

The key line: **integration means coordinating channels around the customer and the marketing objective, not merely using many channels.** Being everywhere is not integration; having each channel do a defined job at the right stage is.

## Measuring by objective

Each objective has its own metrics. **Awareness**: reach, impressions and new visitors. **Consideration**: product-page and review engagement, add-to-cart. **Conversion**: conversion rate, **cost per acquisition** (CPA: ad or marketing spend divided by customers acquired) and **ROAS** (return on ad spend: revenue earned per unit spent on ads). **Retention**: repeat rate, frequency and customer lifetime value. **Advocacy**: review volume and quality, referrals and customer recommendations. The reasoning is that judging a retention tool by impressions, or an awareness campaign by conversions, gives misleading answers.

## Putting it together

The journey gives the stages; the media types give the channels; search advertising shows how paid visibility is bought and ranked; content and app tools build trust and repeat purchase; SoMoLo shows channels reinforcing one another; and measurement ties each activity to the stage and objective it is meant to move. The common thread is that spending is judged by the job done at a stage, not by the number of channels used.
