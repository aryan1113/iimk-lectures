# Session 1: What Exactly Counts as E-Commerce?

## Key Concepts

- **Definition (boundary-based)**: e-commerce is a sale or purchase in which the **order is placed through a computer network using a method designed for ordering**. Scope is broad (goods and services; businesses, consumers, governments, other organisations). Payment and delivery may be online or offline, so the **boundary test** is how the order was placed.
- **E-commerce vs e-business**: e-commerce is a *transaction*, a digitally ordered exchange of goods or services across organisational or market boundaries. E-business is the *wider organisational system*: digital technologies used to run and coordinate processes whether or not a sale occurs (inventory forecasting is e-business; an app grocery order is e-commerce). The customer sees the transaction; managers must design the larger system.
- **The order defines e-commerce, but managers must understand the whole journey**: discovery, payment, fulfilment and service can cross online and offline spaces. Order (website, app, EDI, message, agent) sits at the boundary; discovery (search, social, creator, store visit), payment (UPI, card, wallet, cash on delivery), fulfilment (download, pickup, courier) and service and repeat purchase surround it.
- **Boundary cases (EC / Not EC / Depends)**: apply one test, "was an order for a good or service placed through a digital network?" **Do not confuse digital discovery or digital payment with digital ordering**, nor "digital" with "commercial". Examples: Instagram discovery then a phone call and UPI payment is not a digital order; a ride booked in an app is e-commerce even though the service is delivered offline; a UPI transfer between friends exchanges no good or service. For each case give the judgement, the test and **one missing fact that could change the answer**. (No answer key in the slides.)
- **Eight distinctive characteristics**: *ubiquity* (marketspace, lower transaction costs and cognitive effort); *global reach* (no geographic barriers); *universal standards* (lower entry and search costs, price discovery); *information richness* (detailed content); *interactivity* (real-time two-way communication); *information density* (price transparency, less information asymmetry); *personalisation/customisation* (messages and products adapt to behaviour); *social technology* (user-created content, network effects for distribution).
- **The storefront is only the visible edge.** Visible: search, product, price, order, payment, delivery, service. Beneath it sits **strategy and business model** (what value, for whom, how captured: customer, value proposition, revenue model, competitive position, capabilities, partners), executed by five systems:
  - **Enable**: infrastructure and transactions (platforms, payments, security, fulfilment).
  - **Reach**: marketing and acquisition (search, social, content, offers).
  - **Measure**: data and analytics (behaviour, metrics, attribution, retention intelligence).
  - **Design**: experience and service (journey, usability, accessibility, trust, recovery).
  - **Govern**: consequences and accountability (privacy, fairness, consumer protection, responsibility).
  **Lesson: the storefront displays the promise; strategy defines it, and the organisation delivers it.**
- **Customer simplicity depends on organisational complexity.** Seeing "in stock" requires inventory accuracy and real-time sync; a delivery time requires capacity, routing and operational promises; a review score requires aggregation, moderation and anti-fraud controls; a refund requires policy, exception handling, cash flow and accountability.
- **"Buy now" test**: price correct, stock exists, delivery feasible, payment authorised, fraud risk acceptable, order routed, confirmation sent, failures recoverable. Few of these are visible; the rest are assumptions the firm must make true.

## Why This Matters

It sets the course lens: judge e-commerce by how the order is placed, and look beneath the interface at the system that delivers the promise.

## Relevance for Exams / Cases

Define e-commerce vs e-business, classify a boundary case with the order test and name the fact that would flip it, and trace a visible customer action to the capability behind it.
