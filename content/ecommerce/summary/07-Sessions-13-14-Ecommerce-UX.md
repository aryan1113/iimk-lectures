# Sessions 13-14: E-Commerce UX (Research and Interface Design)

## Key Concepts

- **From analytics to a design problem**: analytics asks **"where?"** (high exits, abandoned carts); UX research asks **"why?"** (interviews, observation, support logs, reviews reveal motivations and uncertainties); design asks **"what next?"** (redesign, then test whether effort and risk fell). Analytics is a clue, not the full explanation. Path: analytics, UX as strategy, journey mapping, user behaviour, persona and scenario, PoV and HMW.
- **UX as business strategy**: it shapes **conversion** (can shoppers complete the task), **trust**, **effort** and **retention**. A better interface improves customer and business value without manipulation, exclusion or distrust.
- **Journey as spine**: awareness, search, evaluate, cart, pay, deliver, support. Look for friction at each handoff (unclear intent, poor findability, unexpected cost, payment anxiety, delivery uncertainty, return difficulty).
- **Double Diamond**: *Discover* (diverge: many signals from analytics, interviews, observation, reviews); *Define* (converge: one problem via scenario, insight, PoV, HMW); *Develop* (diverge: many ideas); *Deliver* (converge: prototype, test, one tested response). Explore widely before defining; test before implementing; avoids solving the wrong problem (e.g. users leave when delivery charges appear, so show total cost earlier).
- **Discovery methods answer different questions**: analytics, what happened at scale; interviews, why (meaning); observation, what users actually do (context); surveys, how common (prevalence); reviews and support logs, existing pain points. Interviews explain what surveys only measure.
- **Persona and scenario**: a persona covers goal, context and constraints, confidence and risk perception, device and language, decision criteria and friction points; design for meaningful differences, not decorative stereotypes. A **scenario** turns a persona into a concrete, testable task. Chain: persona, scenario, goal, one friction, PoV, HMW.
- **PoV**: "[User] needs a way to [need] because [insight]" centres the team. **HMW**: "How might we help [user] [goal] while reducing [friction]?" invites many solutions without prescribing the interface; specific enough to guide, open enough for alternatives. "Improve buying online" is too broad; "add a comparison table" already prescribes a solution.
- **Choose the method by the question**: interface violations, heuristic evaluation; can users complete a task, usability test; compare designs, A/B test; terminology and expectations, interview or card sorting; inclusion, accessibility audit plus user testing; post-launch behaviour, analytics and funnel review. First ask what decision the evidence serves.
- **Usability test**: can the intended user complete a realistic task without help? Give a task, observe with think-aloud and no coaching, then decide on one next step (revise, investigate, larger test). A small user test gives an initial signal, not launch evidence.
- **Nielsen heuristics as audit lens** (experts review a prototype, citing specific features): visibility of system status (loading, order status, payment progress); match with the real world (buyer language, not catalogue labels); user control and freedom (edit cart, undo); error prevention (clear forms, stock and payment checks); plus consistency, recognition over recall, flexibility and efficiency, minimalist design (without removing needed explanations), error recovery, help. Audit output: issue, impact, fix, metric. **Medium-fidelity prototype**: a static mock-up cannot prove totals update or controls work; that needs an interactive prototype.
- **UX laws as design questions**: **Hick's** (too many choices at once? group and prioritise); **Jakob's** (violating familiar conventions? unfamiliar interactions need clear value); **Fitts's** (are key actions large and reachable on mobile? avoid unwanted actions, e.g. "remove" next to "save"); **Tesler's** (where should unavoidable complexity be handled? the system should absorb it, e.g. a logistics zone code).

## Why This Matters

UX research turns analytics signals into the right problem and a tested fix, linking interface choices to trust, effort, conversion and retention.

## Relevance for Exams / Cases

Move from a funnel leak to persona, scenario, PoV and HMW; pick an evaluation method; audit a screen with heuristics and UX laws; explain the Double Diamond.
