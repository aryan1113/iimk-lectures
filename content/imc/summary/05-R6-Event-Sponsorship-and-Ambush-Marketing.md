# R6: Event Sponsorship and Ambush Marketing: Lessons from the Beijing Olympics

Pitt, Parent, Berthon and Steyn, Business Horizons (2010).

## Key Concepts

- **Argument**: event sponsorship is costly and exposes the sponsor to competitor ambush. The "Li Ning affair" at Beijing 2008 shows an ambusher can win more recognition and goodwill than the paying sponsor, yielding seven lessons.
- **Sponsorship cost**: fees run to hundreds of millions for exclusive rights, and **activation** (promotions, TV ads, hospitality) can multiply the budget two or three times: "it is not what the company has, it is what it does with it."
- **Ambush marketing** (Bayless, 1988) = purposeful, false association by a non-sponsor to gain benefits like official sponsors. Narrow sense (Schmitz): direct attack on a competitor's official sponsorship; broad: capitalising on an event's goodwill without authorisation. Example: AMEX's "you don't need a visa" ads against Visa's Olympic sponsorship.
- **Why ambush works**: **schemas** (frameworks shaping memory, so consumers may file the ambusher under the category); **ambivalence** (simultaneous positive and negative attitudes, so heavy-handed enforcement can let the negative win, as fans forced to remove a brewery's trousers disliked official sponsor Budweiser); **salience** (unexpected, hard-to-ignore events capture limited attention, outweighing ethics).
- **Li Ning affair**: Li Ning, China's most decorated 1984 gymnast and founder of a sportswear firm, lit the Olympic cauldron before about 4 billion viewers. Adidas, not Li Ning's firm, was the official footwear sponsor. Called "the boldest case of ambush marketing ever pulled off".
- **Study**: survey of Chinese adults right after the Games tested whether people could identify sponsors vs non-sponsors, brand attitude, and recommendation likelihood. For beer, credit cards and airlines the sponsor was recognised and scored higher. In footwear, Adidas had no attitude edge over Nike, most respondents wrongly thought Li Ning was a sponsor, and Li Ning's recommendation likelihood beat the sponsor's.
- **Li Ning effect** = when a brand ambushes a sponsoring competitor and gets higher sponsorship recognition than the true sponsor, affecting attitude and recommendation.
- **Seven lessons**:
  1. **If an ambush were obvious, it would not be an ambush.** It is unforeseeable (Puma contact lenses at Atlanta); Adidas scanned the wrong direction. Best defence: cover contingencies in the contract.
  2. **Do not expect the property to look after you.** The IOC gave no warning; organisers depend more on broadcasters than sponsors, and a record 63 sponsors risks getting lost in the crowd. Leaving ambush control solely to the property is abdication.
  3. **Do not rely on government or law.** IPR enforcement in China is weak, and suing would mean suing the Chinese Olympic Committee, essentially a government body.
  4. **You knew you might be ambushed.** Adidas predicted it a year earlier but was lulled by official assurances.
  5. **Consumers do not care.** Many see ambush as fair game; local loyalists may cheer a local hero.
  6. **Overreacting makes ambushees look like bullies.** Adidas stayed restrained, probably wisely.
  7. **Sponsorship alone will not do the marketing job.** It needs supporting communication spend and synchronisation with other marketing; if the whole budget goes to sponsorship, returns are not maximised.
- **Guidelines**: do not naively put yourself in line for ambush, but do not abstain; expect an ambush. If ambushed: (1) sue if payoff is positive and costs minimal, (2) use the visibility to reinforce your core message, or (3) do nothing and avoid worsening it.
- **Conclusion**: Li Ning was the clear footwear winner at minimal cost; ambush remains effective.

## Why This Matters

Sponsorship rights do not guarantee owning the association in consumers' minds; it must be defended and integrated with wider communication.

## Relevance for Exams / Cases

Define ambush marketing and the Li Ning effect, explain why ambush works, apply the seven lessons, advise on sponsoring and responding.
