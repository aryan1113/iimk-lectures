# Sessions 4-6: Understanding the Customer

The previous block ended with "who is your customer?" This block works toward an answer using exercises built around Cadbury Dairy Milk and Coke versus Pepsi. It covers customer segmentation, the motivation process, projective techniques, the three components of attitude, how customers differ, and a model of consumer behaviour. It closes with the learning "Investigate before you show your capabilities."

## Starting point

The deck opens with the same material as Session 1-3: the "sell me a pen" exercise, empathy and need to conquer as the essentials of a good salesperson, hard vs soft sell, and the questions "Who is your customer? How do you segment your customers?"

> **Extra context (not on the slides):** Plain-English gloss: segmenting means splitting customers into groups that share needs or traits so you can serve each differently.

## "Each customer is different"

The slide asks "Agree or disagree? Why?" It gives no answer.

## The motivation process

The slide shows a flow: an **unfulfilled goal or need** (needs, wants, desires) creates **tension**, which **drives behaviour**, leading to **fulfilment** and **tension reduction**. **Personality, perception, learning and attitudes** are shown alongside the process as influences.

> **Extra context (not on the slides):** Think of hunger: you feel hungry, restless, buy a snack, and feel relieved. A seller can look for the tension a customer already feels.

## Projective techniques with Cadbury Dairy Milk

The slide sets the scene: "You are selling Cadbury Chocolates. Describe your customer." Four prompts follow.

> **In class:** (1) **Animal analogy**: "If Cadbury Dairy Milk was an animal, what animal would it be?" (2) **Word association test**: "What are the words you would associate with Cadbury Dairy Milk?" (3) **Sentence completion**: "People who buy Dairy Milk are...", "Chocolate is...", "I eat Dairy Milk when...", "If my friend gives me a chocolate, I...", "If people are concerned about the quality of Cadbury, they..." (4) **Picture interpretation**: a picture with "What is she thinking?" and "What is the personality of the customer buying these?" A further slide asks "Describe the taste of Coke and Pepsi." The slides give no answers.

> **Extra context (not on the slides):** These are examples of what are usually called projective techniques: prompts that let people express views indirectly. The slides do not use the label or explain why they are used; a common reason is that direct questions often give polite or shallow answers. Different prompts suit different goals (for example animal and picture prompts for personality, word association for brand image).

## Attitude

The slide asks "What is your customer's attitude towards your product? Where does attitude come from: heart, brain or habit?" It then shows three components:

| Component | Label on the slide |
|---|---|
| **Cognitive** | Think |
| **Affective** | Feel |
| **Conative** | Act |

> **Extra context (not on the slides):** Our reading is that heart ties to affective, brain to cognitive, habit to conative. The three parts can disagree: someone may think a brand is overpriced, love it anyway, and buy it out of habit. The slides give no example.

## How customers differ

The summary slide says customers differ in **demographics** (age, gender, education, training, experience, income) and **psychological factors** (motivation, perception, attitude, personality, learning, memory).

## Model of consumer behaviour

The slide diagram, read left to right: **marketing stimuli** (product, price, distribution, communication) and **other stimuli** (economic, technological, political, cultural) pass through **consumer psychology** (motivation, perception, learning, memory) and **consumer characteristics** (cultural, social, personal), into the **buying decision process** and then the **purchase decision**.

> **Extra context (not on the slides):** The takeaway is that the same marketing push can give different results depending on what happens inside the buyer and in their social setting.

## Back to the pen, and the learning

The slide asks "Would you now sell the pen any differently?" It gives no answer, and links two "sell me this pen" videos (their content is not on the slides). The closing learning is stated: **"Investigate before you show your capabilities."**

## Putting it together

The block builds a diagnostic toolkit: segmentation, the motivation process, projective prompts, three attitude components and the consumer behaviour model. The one stated rule is to investigate before presenting capabilities.

## Likely exam questions

1. **Apply the consumer behaviour model to a product.** List marketing and other stimuli, consumer psychology and characteristics, then the decision process, for that product.
2. **Use a projective technique for a product.** Name one of the four (animal, word association, sentence completion, picture) and write the prompt. Why it works is our gloss (outside the slides).
3. **Explain the three attitude components with an example.** Cognitive (think), affective (feel), conative (act), with your own example.
4. **Describe the motivation process.** Need or goal, tension, behaviour, fulfilment and tension reduction, with personality, perception, learning and attitudes as influences.
5. **What is the closing learning of the block?** "Investigate before you show your capabilities"; link it forward to SPIN (outside the slides).
