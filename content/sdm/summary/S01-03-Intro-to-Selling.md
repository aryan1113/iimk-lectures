# Sessions 1-3: Introduction to Selling

## Key Concepts

- **Course map**: Module 1 Selling, then Negotiation/Networks/Persuasion, Salesforce Management, and Distribution Channels.
- **Why selling matters**: every business model needs both *economic logic* and *value creation*; selling is required in any profession, and great leaders are often great salespeople.
- **Evolution of business orientation**: production → selling → product → market/customer → **experience orientation** ("experience and engagement economy"). Customers now judge the whole journey: pre-purchase, purchase, post-purchase.
- **Market dynamics (VUCA)**: volatility, uncertainty, complexity, ambiguity push organisations toward agility, ambidexterity, lean thinking, open-mindedness and an ecosystem mindset; at product level this means user-driven, dynamic product development, SaaS and omnichannel.
- **Re-imagining the customer experience**: build a persona (needs, challenges, opportunities), an empathy map, then map the customer journey phases (activities, touchpoints, thoughts, sentiment) and redesign one touchpoint.
- **Myths about selling**: salespeople are born not made; good talkers; can sell anything to anyone; fun, games and travel; "get rich quick"; people don't want to buy.
- **Why people choose sales**: high earnings, quick recognition, freedom, mobility. Career paths: career salesperson, sales management, general management, entrepreneurship.
- **What makes a good salesperson**: empathy plus a need to conquer; the "sell me a pen" exercise shows that jumping to the pitch fails.
- **Hard vs soft sell**: hard sell = concern for self, talking, pushing product, presenting features; soft sell = concern for customer, questions, listening, presenting benefits, acknowledging needs.

## Why This Matters

Sets the mindset for the whole course: selling is a customer-centred, investigative process, not persuasion by talking. The orientation history explains why modern B2B and B2C selling emphasise experience and empathy.

## Relevance for Exams / Cases

Likely uses: classify a firm's orientation, critique a customer journey, contrast hard vs soft selling, and refute sales myths with reasons.
