# Sessions 13-15: Negotiation

## Key Concepts

- **Negotiation** is how people deal with differences. Two paths: **distributive** (fixed pie, win-lose, "who claims the most"; seller wants high, buyer low) and **integrative** (cooperate to integrate interests, create value *and* claim it). The negotiator's dilemma is choosing between creating and claiming value.
- **Distributive keys**: start from a strong position, take-it-or-leave-it, high/low-balling. Against hard bargainers, stay prepared with your alternatives rather than matching their stance.
- **Integrative keys**: value creation, BATNA, reservation price, ZOPA.
  - **Value creation**: find options that give you a gain and cost the other side nothing: more quantity, payment options, value adds, free services, back-end discounts, annual maintenance contracts. With a premium product (Apple) you *highlight* value; where a rival matches price (Samsung vs Redmi, local store vs Chroma) you must *create* value. Trade on differing priorities (donkey-and-horse exercise: customer wants 25 blue pens, you want to sell 40 sketch pens). Do it ethically. Bottom line: find a value, make a sale.
  - **BATNA** = best alternative to a negotiated agreement (e.g. a consultant's other Rs 4 lakh assignment sets their floor). Improve your position: know yours, improve it, identify the other side's, weaken theirs, offer a win-win proposition.
  - **Reservation price** = walk-away price, the least favourable point you accept; derived from BATNA but not always equal.
  - **ZOPA** = zone of possible agreement, the overlap between both sides' reservation prices.
- **Five styles** (Lewicki & Hiam): *competition* (win-lose, outcome over relationship, distributive); *collaboration* (win-win, high on outcome and relationship, integrative, costs more effort); *compromise* (split the difference, between distributive and integrative); *accommodation* (lose-win, you accept a distributive outcome to protect the future relationship); *avoidance* (lose-lose, neither outcome nor relationship matters). Integrative = collaborating/compromising; distributive = competing/accommodating.
- **Communication**: politeness, humility, product awareness, soft speech with persuasive arguments, always speak for the customer. Best deals come when both sides think about the other's value. Speak the same language when selling; avoid mirroring it when buying.
- **Tactics: name them and counter them**
  - *Foot-in-the-door*: small request first, then scale up. *Door-in-the-face*: unreasonable request first, then a reasonable one.
  - *Take-it-or-leave-it (the brink)*: for hard bargaining.
  - *Bogey*: pretend an issue matters to you so the other side feels they must reciprocate.
  - *Nibble*: after the deal is nearly done, ask a small unmentioned concession when the other side is worn out.
  - *Snow job*: overwhelm with information so they lose sight of what matters.
  - *Lack of authority*: "I/they cannot decide". *Deadlines*: create time pressure.
  - *High/low-balling*: open extreme to shift the other side's reservation price; counter by calling it out, asking for justification, or using BATNA. The most common tactic in the AMA survey.
  - *Good cop/bad cop*: in a two-person team one is reasonable, the other is not.
  - *Make it personal*: use personal experience and emotion. *Ask questions*: learn their motives and buy thinking time.
  - *Behavioural levers*: anchoring, mental accounting, prospect theory, decoy and endowment effects.
- **Role play checklist**: negotiation skills (emphasise value, think for the other party, move toward integrative), preparedness (BATNA, ZOPA, walk-away price), communication, adaptability and perseverance.
- **Takeaway**: "Value creation, value creation, value creation"; use BATNA, ZOPA and reservation price to build value propositions.

## Why This Matters

Negotiation is where value is split; the course stresses that preparation (BATNA/ZOPA) and creative value creation beat hard bargaining.

## Relevance for Exams / Cases

Identify ZOPA from two reservation prices, name the tactic in a vignette and counter it, and recommend a negotiation style with justification.
